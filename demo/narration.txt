[03] (shot2-live-scores.png) Perfect Corp's engine scores eight concerns. Mine came back with redness at fifty-five, pores at sixty-six, and radiance at seventy-two as the weakest areas - an overall skin score of seventy-four, and an estimated skin age of thirty-one.
[04] (shot3-routine.png) The agent ranks those concerns weakest-first and builds a morning and evening routine around them. Every card names the product, the price, and the reason it earned its place - the first treatment aims straight at redness, my weakest score.
[05] (shot3b-tryon.png) Tinted finishers carry a try-it-on button for YouCam's makeup virtual try-on. In this build that step is a labeled placeholder - the try-on task requires a public image U R L, and a private selfie stays private. Nothing is faked on screen.
[06] (shot4-refine.png) Then I push back: under twenty dollars each, and everything fragrance-free. The routine rebuilds, and the agent shows its receipt - seven changes, with every swap named, and the basket falling from a hundred and fifty dollars to sixty-six.
[07] (shot1-hero-photo.png) Under the hood it is deliberately boring: zero-dependency Python, a deterministic agent with no L L M bill and no nondeterminism - and an honesty rule baked in. Sample data is always labeled as sample, never dressed up as a measurement.
